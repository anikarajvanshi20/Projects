How does national well-being Shape Global Music Consumption in 2024?

The 3 datasets used:

  • Global streaming data from spotify for 2024

  • World happiness report 2024

  • World happiness report 2023

Hypothesis

H1: Populations facing higher economic uncertainty will exhibit higher levels of repetitive music consumption.

Rationale: Research shows that individuals often prefer familiar music over new music (Pereira et al., 2011). This implies in times of stress and unpredictability, individuals will choose more familiar music rather than experiment. This was quantified through assessing how many times songs were repeatedly streamed.

Independent Variable: Economic Uncertainty Index

Dependent Variable: Spotify Repetition Score (Streams per Listener)

H2: Populations with lower levels of social support and higher economic uncertainty will consume a higher proportion of positive music.

Rationale: This hypothesis was based on research conducted by de Lucio & Palomeque (2024) who found that when there is economic uncertainty and low social support, individuals lack external resources for mood regulation and rely on positive music instead. There exists a relatively novel term for this phenomenon known as recession pop which suggests that the rise of consumption in upbeat or pop music can be a recession indicator (Dickler & Solá, 2024).

Independent Variable: Economic Uncertainty Index and Social Support Index

Dependent Variable: Positive Music Share

H3: Higher economic uncertainty correlates with a preference for older music.

Rationale: Uncertainty drives a psychological retreat into the safe past. Listeners in uncertain economies will stream music with older average release years to evoke simpler, more stable times. Gibbs & Egermann (2021) found that nostalgic music boosts positive emotions and can be used as an emotion regulation technique.

Independent Variable: Economic Uncertainty Index

Dependent Variable: Nostalgia Index

Motivation

To test the three hypothesis established by research above where populations facing economic uncertainty or low social support regulate their mood through specific media consumption habits for the year 2024.

Data Wrangling

  1. The two happiness report datasets from 2023 and 2024 were joined using a full join to ensure no country was dropped in case of minor mismatches across reports. The suffix was changed from x and y to make it easier to handle later and clearer to understand.
joined_whr <- full_join(x = happiness_report_2023, 
                        y = happiness_report_2024, 
                        by = "Country.name",
                        suffix = c(".2023", ".2024"))

2.The genres in the spotify dataset were converted to factor. Then using the unique function, all the genres were assesed. They were then classified into two categories upbeat music and mellow music. These categories were saved as individual values. Then a new column was added called category and the respective categories were added using case when.Then the columns that were not needed removed.

spotify_dataset$Genre <- as.factor(spotify_dataset$Genre)

spotify_dataset_modified <- spotify_dataset %>% 
  mutate(Category = case_when(
    Genre %in% Upbeat_genres ~ "Upbeat",
    Genre %in% Mellow_genres ~ "Mellow",
    TRUE ~ "Other"
  )) %>% 
  select(!c(Artist, Album, Avg.Stream.Duration..Min.))
  1. The dependent variables were constructed from the spotify dataset by creating 4 scores. The data was grouped by country. The first the repetition score to test comfort listening. This was calculated by diving the sum of the total streams by the sum of monthly listeners. Then the nostalgia index was calculated by taking difference between the weighted mean if the release year in respect to the total hours streamed and 2024. The avg skip rate was calculted by the weighted mean of the skip rate against the total streams. Finally, the positive share was calculated as the proportion of total listening hours devoted to upbeat music.
metrics <- spotify_dataset_modified %>%
  group_by(Country) %>%
  summarise(
    Repetition_Score = sum(Total_Streams_Millions) /
  sum(Monthly_Listeners_Millions),
    nostalgia_index = 2024 - weighted.mean(Release_Year, w = Total_hours_Streamed),
    Avg_Skip_Rate = weighted.mean(Skip_Rate, w = Total_Streams_Millions),
    Positive_Hours = sum(Total_hours_Streamed[Category == "Upbeat"])/
    sum(Total_hours_Streamed),
    .groups = "drop")

  1. A z function was created that scaled the values to create the indices. The differences were scaled using the z function. For worsened outcomes the scaling was inversed to better construct the economic uncertainty index. Then the indices were created by first binding the columns for the parameters of interest and then finding their row means. Then the country names and the calculated indices were selected for further analysis.
z <- function(x) {
  as.numeric(scale(x))
}

whr_indices <- whr_changes %>% 
  mutate(z_gdp = z(x = delta_gdp),
         z_gdp_worsen = - z(x = delta_gdp),
         z_corruption_worsen = - z(x = delta_corruption),
         z_dystopia_worsen = - z(x = delta_dystopia),
         z_social_support = z(x = delta_social_support),
         z_generosity = z(x = delta_generosity),
         z_hle = z(x = delta_hle),
         z_freedom = z(x = delta_freedom),
         economics_uncertainty_index = rowMeans(
           cbind(z_gdp_worsen,z_corruption_worsen, z_dystopia_worsen)), 
         social_support_index = rowMeans(
           cbind(z_social_support, z_generosity)), 
         life_eval_index = rowMeans(
           cbind(z_hle, z_freedom))) %>%
  select(Country.name, economics_uncertainty_index, 
         social_support_index, life_eval_index)

Conclusion

To conclude, the study examined how shifts in national well-being relate to global music consumption patterns for the year 2024. While economic uncertainty showed a positive relationship with repetitive listening, this association was not statistically significant, providing only directional but not inferential support for H1. Partial support was found for H2, as higher economic uncertainty was associated with a greater share of positive music consumption at a trend level, and lower social support showed a weak negative association with positive music share, together suggesting that upbeat music may serve as a compensatory emotional resource during socioeconomic stress. Contrary to H3, economic uncertainty was significantly associated with lower nostalgia, indicating a shift toward newer rather than older music during periods of instability. This could be due to how nostalgia was operationalized, as the dataset only included songs released between 2018 and 2023. Overall, these findings suggest that national well-being is meaningfully linked to collective music consumption patterns and that genre-level listening behavior may offer a tentative behavioral marker of socioeconomic conditions.
